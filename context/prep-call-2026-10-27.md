## What the panel looks for
- **Technical depth matters most** ("the goal with solutions is to find folks that are inherently
  technical"): accuracy, sample count, speed.
- **Communication**: pitched as if to a customer. Don't dumb down, but don't assume everyone knows
  every concept.
- **Story framework the team uses for discovery and demos: SEE -> UNDERSTAND -> ACT.**
  - See: what the camera / model has to identify (food blobs + plate).
  - Understand: what gets computed (% eaten per item, vs before photo).
  - Act: the output the customer acts on (chart entry, low-intake alert).
- **ROI in dollars** for the buyer, vs how they operate today.
- **Production variant**: constraints, limits, hardware per home, upfront cost.
- **Active Learning loop**: how the model would be iterated after deployment.
- **Beyond this use case**: other clients / industries the same build fits.
- Known gaps and edge cases: name them and the next step for each.

## Warnings
- **Own every slide.** A past candidate could not explain hardware on his own (AI-made) slide.
  AI is fine for visuals; the content must be understood and added by the presenter.
- **Record a backup video today** of the demo working. "The live demo curse is 100% a thing."
  Default to showing it live.
- Expect pushback / edge cases ("what can we do to make it better?"). Be receptive, not defensive.
- A follow-up task from Nick ("go fix or try X") is usually a **good** sign: it tests fast iteration.
